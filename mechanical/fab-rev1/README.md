## Ordering at SendCutSend

1. **Upload both DXFs.** Drop them together — they're priced per part.
2. **Material:** *Aluminum Composite (ACM)* → **0.118″ (3 mm)** → **matte black**.
3. **Quantity:** P1 × 1, **P2 × 2**. The second button plate is a spare — it's the

4. **Secondary services:** none. ACM can't be bent, tapped, countersunk or

Both parts are inside SendCutSend's 30 × 44″ maximum.

## What the drawings assume

- **The button centreline lands 25.44″ below the top of the face plate.** With the
  kiosk bottom at 34.75″ AFF that puts the buttons at **38″ AFF** — the ADA datum
  the whole design is placed from. Don't change the face plate height without
  redoing that arithmetic.
- **The screen window is deliberately generous** — 0.25″ oversize per side.
  “24 inch” covers 23.6″–24.0″ diagonals, and this window clears all of them
  without cropping a pixel. The margin falls on the monitor's own black bezel
  (0.35″ minimum), so behind a black face plate nothing shows.
- **The monitor is aligned to the window, not the other way round.** It hangs on a
  VESA plate on an internal frame we build, so any model-specific offset between
  a monitor's active area and its VESA pattern gets taken up there. That's why
  P1 is safe to cut before the monitor arrives.
- **The button plate sits behind the face plate**, its 0.75″ lip captured from the
  back, leaving the control strip recessed 3 mm. No fastener is visible from the
  front.

## Before you press order — check one number

**⌀30.5 mm is the hole for a “30 mm” anti-vandal switch.** Most use 30.2–30.5 mm,
and cutting the larger size is the safe direction (the nut and washer cover the
slack). But confirm it against the datasheet of the switch you're actually
buying, along with:

- **panel thickness range includes 3 mm** — 3 mm ACM clears every switch we found,
  but check
- **actuation force ≤ 5 lbf** — ADA §309.4

If your switch wants a different cutout, change `BTN_HOLE` in the script and
re-run. That's the only number here tied to a part we haven't bought yet.

## Labels

There's no text in the DXFs. BACK / HOME / NEXT is a separate step, and the
native answer for ACM is **cut vinyl from a sign shop** — this is sign material,
and it's how every road sign is lettered. Durable, cheap, replaceable, and it
survives outdoor weather, never mind a museum. Ask SendCutSend about engraving
first; if they'll do it, that's better still.

## Regenerating

```bash
python3 make-cutfiles.py
```

thickness, minimum hole size, and that the button datum still lands at 25.440″.
